## 2. Unit & Course Outcome Audit

### AAS101E / AAS201E — Quantum Physics and Applications
- **Short Title**: Quantum Physics | **Category**: BSC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 3 | **NCrF Hours**: 45
- **Semesters**: Semester 1, Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand the foundational concepts and principles of quantum mechanics.
  - Understand the quantum mechanical principles governing the electronic properties of solids.
  - Comprehend the fundamental principles of quantum computation and communication in various quantum information processing systems.
  - Comprehend the principles and applications of photonics.
  - Explore the fundamental properties and phenomena of superconductivity and diverse applications of nano-materials in advanced technological contexts.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K2): Understand the foundational concepts and principles of quantum mechanics.
  - **CO2** (K2): Understand the quantum mechanical principles governing the electronic properties of solids.
  - **CO3** (K3): Comprehend the fundamental principles of quantum computation and communication in various quantum information processing systems.
  - **CO4** (K3): Comprehend the principles and applications of photonics.
  - **CO5** (K2): Explore the fundamental properties and phenomena of superconductivity and nano-materials.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Fundamentals of Quantum Mechanics** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Inadequacy of classical mechanics, de-Broglie concept of matter waves, Davisson and Germer experiment, Wave packet, phase velocity, group velocity, Heisenberg's uncertainty principle and its applications
    - *Real-world Applications*: Nanoscale device modeling, Quantum wells, Electron tunneling
  - **Unit 2: Quantum Theory of Solids** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Free electron theory, Fermi-Dirac distribution, Energy bands in solids, band gap, Conductors, semiconductors and insulators, Effective mass, density of states
    - *Real-world Applications*: Semiconductor physics, Transistor design, Solid state electronics
  - **Unit 3: Quantum Computing and Communication** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Quantum computation and quantum states, Quantum superposition, quantum entanglement, decoherence, Quantum bits (qubits), physical realization of qubits (solid state, photonics), Basics of quantum gates (single-qubit gate, multi-qubit gate), Quantum cryptography, quantum key distribution (QKD)
    - *Real-world Applications*: Quantum cryptography (QKD), Quantum logic circuits, Secure quantum communication
  - **Unit 4: Photonics** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Fiber optics: Fundamental idea about optical fiber, Acceptance angle, acceptance cone, numerical aperture, Principle of light propagation in optical fiber, Attenuation and attenuation coefficient in fibre, Laser: Interaction of radiation with matter (absorption, spontaneous and stimulated emission)
    - *Real-world Applications*: High-speed optical fiber communications, Laser surgery & cutting, Optical sensors
  - **Unit 5: Superconductors and Nano-materials** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Superconductors: Temperature dependence of resistivity and critical field, Meissner effect, Type I and Type II superconductors, persistent current, High temperature superconductors, properties, applications, Nano-materials: Introduction and properties of nano-materials, Basic concept of quantum dots, quantum wires and quantum well
    - *Real-world Applications*: MRI machines, Maglev trains, Quantum dot displays, Lossless power transmission
- **Textbooks & References**:
  - A. Beiser, Concepts of Modern Physics, McGraw Hill Education
  - G. Keiser, Optical Fiber Communications, McGraw Hill
  - M. A. Nielsen & I. L. Chuang, Quantum Computation and Quantum Information, Cambridge University Press
  - C. Kittel, Introduction to Solid State Physics, Wiley
  - S. O. Pillai, Solid State Physics, New Age International

### AAS102D / AAS202D — Applied Chemistry for Smart Systems
- **Short Title**: Applied Chemistry | **Category**: BSC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 3 | **NCrF Hours**: 45
- **Semesters**: Semester 1, Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand the chemistry of electronic and computing materials underpinning modern hardware.
  - Apply electrochemical principles to analyze energy storage and power systems for smart devices.
  - Study the chemistry of sensors, biosensors, and chemical detection systems used in smart technologies.
  - Analyze the chemistry of nanomaterials and functional coatings relevant to smart systems and IoT.
  - Introduce green chemistry principles and sustainable practices in electronics manufacturing and e-waste management.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K2): Understand the chemistry of semiconductor and computing hardware materials.
  - **CO2** (K3): Apply electrochemical principles to analyze energy storage and power systems for smart devices.
  - **CO3** (K4): Analyze chemical sensing principles and apply them to smart system and IoT applications.
  - **CO4** (K2): Understand the chemistry of nanomaterials and functional materials used in smart technologies.
  - **CO5** (K3): Apply green chemistry principles to sustainable electronics design and e-waste management.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Chemistry of Hardware Materials for Computing and Electronic Devices** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Introduction to materials used in computers and electronic devices, Semiconductors and conductors; silicon and its properties, Basic concepts of doping, Printed circuit board (PCB) materials, Soldering materials (lead-free alloys)
    - *Real-world Applications*: PCB fabrication, Dielectric layer optimization, Corrosion prevention in data centers
  - **Unit 2: Energy Storage and Power Chemistry** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Fundamentals of electrochemistry, Primary and secondary batteries, Lithium-ion batteries and their applications in laptops, mobiles and EVs, Battery safety and charging principles, Supercapacitors and fuel cells
    - *Real-world Applications*: EV batteries, Smartphone fast charging, Wearable energy harvesting
  - **Unit 3: Sensors and Chemical Detection** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Principles of chemical sensing, Electrochemical sensors (amperometric, potentiometric), Gas sensors (metal oxide, optical, MEMS-based), Biosensors (enzyme, immunosensor, DNA sensor), RFID tag chemistry
    - *Real-world Applications*: Air quality monitoring, Medical diagnostic biosensors, Smart agricultural soil probes
  - **Unit 4: Nanomaterials and Functional Materials for Smart Systems** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Introduction to nanotechnology, Carbon nanotubes (CNTs) and Graphene, Quantum dots and smart coatings, Self-cleaning and hydrophobic surfaces, Photochromic and thermochromic materials
    - *Real-world Applications*: Foldable screens, Smart wearable sensors, Thermal dissipation coatings
  - **Unit 5: Green Chemistry and E-waste Management** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Principles of green chemistry, Environmentally friendly electronic materials and biodegradable electronics, E-waste generation and hazardous components, Recycling of electronic waste and recovery of valuable metals (Au, Ag, Cu), RoHS and WEEE directives
    - *Real-world Applications*: Urban mining of e-waste, RoHS compliant manufacturing, Eco-friendly component design

  - P. C. Jain & M. Jain, Engineering Chemistry, Dhanpat Rai Publishing
  - B. R. Puri, L. R. Sharma & M. S. Pathania, Principles of Physical Chemistry, Vishal Publishing

- **Course Objectives**:
  - Apply multivariable calculus and optimization techniques to solve engineering problems in AI, machine learning, and cloud computing.
  - Apply multiple integrals to analyze multidimensional data, probability distributions, and image processing applications.
  - Use vector calculus concepts to solve problems in robotics, computer graphics, AI, and engineering simulations.
  - Apply matrix algebra and numerical methods to solve computational problems in machine learning, graphics, and data analysis.
  - Understand vector spaces and apply them to data representation, signal processing, dimensionality reduction, and data compression.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K3): Apply multivariable calculus for optimization in AI, ML, and cloud computing.
  - **CO2** (K3): Apply multiple integrals to analyze multidimensional data and image processing applications.
  - **CO3** (K3): Use vector calculus concepts to solve problems in robotics, computer graphics, and AI.
  - **CO4** (K3): Apply matrix algebra and eigenvalue analysis to solve computational problems in ML and graphics.
  - **CO5** (K2): Understand vector spaces and apply them to data representation and dimensionality reduction.

  - **Unit 1: Calculus** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Limits, continuity and differentiability for function of two variables, Partial derivatives, composite functions, total derivative, Euler's Theorem for homogeneous functions, Change of variables, expansion for function of two variables, Jacobians and properties of Jacobian (without proof)
    - *Real-world Applications*: AI model optimization, Cloud computing resource allocation, Neural network loss tuning

    - *Core Topics*: Evaluation of double and triple integrals, Change of order of integration, Change of variable (double integral), Beta and Gamma functions, properties of Beta and Gamma function, Dirichlet's Integral

    - *Real-world Applications*: AI heat mapping, Robotics path planning, Computer graphics vector flow fields
  - **Unit 4: Matrices** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Introduction to matrices, Complex matrices (Hermitian, Skew Hermitian, Unitary), Elementary transformations, Inverse by E-transformation, Rank of a matrix by Echelon form, Solution of system of linear equations by direct method (Gauss Elimination) and iterative method (Gauss Seidel), Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors, similarity transformation
    - *Real-world Applications*: Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Face recognition systems, Error detection/correction codes, 3D graphics rendering
  - **Unit 5: Introduction to Vector Space** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Definition of Field and Vector Space, Basic properties of vector spaces, Subspaces, Linear dependence and independence of vectors, Basis and dimension
    - *Real-world Applications*: Engineering data modeling and compression, Signal representation and noise reduction, Feature space projections
- **Textbooks & References**:

### AAS203D — Numerical Methods
- **Short Title**: Numerical Methods | **Category**: BSC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 4 | **NCrF Hours**: 60
- **Semesters**: Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand advanced mathematical concepts used in engineering and computing.
  - Develop analytical and problem-solving skills using differential equations, complex analysis, transforms, and linear algebra.
  - Apply mathematical techniques to model and solve real-world problems in computer science and engineering.
  - Build a strong foundation for advanced studies in data science, artificial intelligence, machine learning, computer graphics, and cybersecurity.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K3): Solve linear differential equations and apply them to model engineering problems.
  - **CO2** (K3): Apply complex variable techniques and contour integration in signal analysis and engineering.
  - **CO3** (K3): Use Laplace transforms to analyze dynamical systems and solve differential equations.
  - **CO4** (K3): Apply Fourier series and Fourier transforms to image compression and signal processing.
  - **CO5** (K3): Apply matrix decompositions (LU, QR, SVD) to machine learning and cryptographic problems.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Differential Equations** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Linear differential equation of nth order with constant coefficients, Cauchy-Euler equation, Solution of second order linear differential equations with variable coefficient by method of variation of parameters, Change of independent variable, Normal form of differential equations
    - *Real-world Applications*: Cloud computing workload modeling, Network traffic flow, Cybersecurity threat diffusion
  - **Unit 2: Complex Analysis** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Functions of complex variables, limit, continuity, differentiability, Analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann Equations (Cartesian and Polar), Harmonic function, Milne-Thomson Method, Complex integrals, Cauchy Integral Theorem and Cauchy Integral Formula (without proof), Taylor's and Laurent's series, singularities, residue of a function
    - *Real-world Applications*: Image processing, Computer algorithm design, Quantum state computation
  - **Unit 3: Laplace Transform** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Definition of Laplace Transform, existence conditions, linearity property, Laplace transform of standard functions, First and Second Shifting theorems, Change of scale property, transforms of derivatives and integrals, Periodic functions, Unit Step function, Inverse Laplace Transform, Partial Fraction Method, Convolution Theorem
    - *Real-world Applications*: Database cache dynamics, Algorithmic delay modeling, Control loops
  - **Unit 4: Fourier Series and Fourier Transform** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Periodic functions, even and odd functions, Dirichlet's conditions, Fourier series expansion in interval c to c+2l, change of interval, Half range series: half range sine and cosine series in -l to l, Parseval's identity, Fourier Integral theorem, Fourier transform, Inverse Fourier Transform, Finite Fourier Sin and Cosine transform
    - *Real-world Applications*: Digital image processing, JPEG compression, ML feature extraction, Audio frequency filtering
  - **Unit 5: Applied Linear Algebra** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Eigenvalue Decomposition and Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), Matrix factorization: LU Decomposition (Doolittle method), QR Decomposition, Linear transformations, matrix representation of linear transformations, Kernel and range of linear transformations, Introduction to inner product spaces, norm of a vector
    - *Real-world Applications*: Latent semantic analysis, Neural network weight factorization, Cryptography, 2D/3D graphics transformations
- **Textbooks & References**:
  - E. Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, John Wiley & Sons
  - B. S. Grewal, Higher Engineering Mathematics, Khanna Publishers
  - David C. Lay, Linear Algebra and its Applications, Pearson
  - J. W. Brown & R. V. Churchill, Complex Variables and Applications, McGraw-Hill

### AEE101A / AEE201A — Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering
- **Short Title**: Electrical Engineering | **Category**: ESC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 3 | **NCrF Hours**: 45
- **Semesters**: Semester 1, Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand basic electrical quantities — voltage, current, resistance, power, and energy — and apply Ohm's law.
  - Apply Kirchhoff's laws and simple network theorems to analyse DC circuits.
  - Understand AC circuits and the concept of power factor in electrical systems.
  - Learn how transformers work and how they are used in everyday power supplies and energy distribution.
  - Understand electrical machines (DC and AC motors) and relate them to emerging applications, solar PV systems, and safety aspects.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K3): Apply Ohm's law and Kirchhoff's laws to analyse basic DC circuits.
  - **CO2** (K3): Analyse single-phase AC circuits and explain power factor and its correction.
  - **CO3** (K3): Analyse balanced three-phase circuits and measure three-phase power.
  - **CO4** (K2): Explain magnetic circuits and transformer working principles and efficiency.
  - **CO5** (K2): Explain operating principles of DC and AC motors and solar PV systems.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Basic Electrical Quantities and DC Circuits** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Electric charge, current, voltage, EMF, resistance; Ohm's law, Resistors in series and parallel; voltage divider and current divider, Power and energy in a DC circuit, Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) and Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL), Simple mesh and nodal analysis
    - *Real-world Applications*: Power distribution in computing server racks, Battery management circuitry
  - **Unit 2: AC Circuits** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Generation of AC voltage; sinusoidal waveform: peak, RMS, average, form factor, Phasor representation; AC through pure R, pure L, pure C, Series RL and RC circuits; impedance, Active power (P), reactive power (Q), apparent power (S), Power factor (PF) and power factor improvement using capacitors
    - *Real-world Applications*: UPS systems for servers, Power factor correction in data centers
  - **Unit 3: Three-Phase Systems** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Advantages of three-phase supply over single-phase, Generation of balanced three-phase voltage, Star (Y) and delta (Delta) connections, Phase and line voltages and currents in balanced circuits, Power in balanced three-phase circuits
    - *Real-world Applications*: Industrial power delivery, Server farm three-phase power distribution
  - **Unit 4: Magnetic Circuits and Transformers** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Magnetic flux, flux density, MMF, reluctance, Simple series magnetic circuit, Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction; self and mutual inductance, Principle of operation of single-phase transformer, EMF equation; ideal transformer: voltage ratio, current ratio, turns ratio
    - *Real-world Applications*: Smartphone chargers / SMPS adapters, Grid substations
  - **Unit 5: Electrical Machines and Emerging Electrical Systems** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: DC motor: principle, back EMF, torque, speed control concept, Types of DC motors and their applications, BLDC motor: basic concept and applications, Three-phase induction motor: rotating magnetic field, slip, torque, Introduction to solar photovoltaic (PV) systems: single diode model, I-V characteristics
    - *Real-world Applications*: Drone motors (BLDC), Electric Vehicles (EV), Solar rooftop microgrids
- **Textbooks & References**:
  - D. P. Kothari & I. J. Nagrath, Basic Electrical Engineering, Tata McGraw Hill
  - V. Del Toro, Electrical Engineering Fundamentals, Prentice Hall
  - B. L. Theraja & A. K. Theraja, Electrical Technology Vol. I, S. Chand
  - L. S. Bobrow, Fundamentals of Electrical Engineering, Oxford University Press

### AEC101A / AEC201A — Fundamentals of Electronics Engineering
- **Short Title**: Electronics Engineering | **Category**: ESC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 3 | **NCrF Hours**: 45
- **Semesters**: Semester 1, Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand p-n junction diode behaviour and apply it in rectifier and voltage regulator circuits.
  - Understand BJT and MOSFET as switches and amplifiers in digital and analog circuits.
  - Understand operational amplifier characteristics and use it in basic signal conditioning circuits.
  - Understand digital logic gates, Boolean algebra, and basic combinational circuits.
  - Relate electronics fundamentals to real products — microcontrollers, IoT devices, smartphones, and embedded systems.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K2): Understand p-n junction diode behaviour and rectifier/regulator circuits.
  - **CO2** (K2): Understand BJT and MOSFET switching and amplification characteristics.
  - **CO3** (K3): Understand op-amp characteristics and basic signal conditioning circuits.
  - **CO4** (K3): Apply Boolean algebra and digital logic design to combinational circuits.
  - **CO5** (K2): Explain microcontroller architecture, sensors, ADC, and wireless communication interfaces.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Diodes and Power Supply Circuits** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: p-n junction: depletion layer, forward and reverse bias, Ideal diode vs real diode, V-I characteristics, diode as a switch, Half-wave and full-wave bridge rectifier, filter capacitor, Zener diode: Zener breakdown, voltage regulator circuit, LED: working and current-limiting resistor calculations
    - *Real-world Applications*: USB-C charger power supply, Reverse polarity protection, Power status indicators
  - **Unit 2: Transistors as Switches and Amplifiers** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT): Construction & VI characteristics, NPN and PNP, Three regions of operation: cut-off, active, saturation, BJT as a switch (digital control of relay, buzzer, motor), BJT as a common-emitter amplifier (concept and gain), Introduction to FETs and MOSFET: N-channel MOSFET, gate-source voltage, MOSFET as switch
    - *Real-world Applications*: Motor speed control (PWM), Microcontroller relay drivers, Audio amplifiers
  - **Unit 3: Operational Amplifier (Op-Amp)** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Ideal op-amp characteristics: infinite gain, infinite input impedance, zero output impedance, Practical characteristics of 741 IC, Inverting amplifier and non-inverting amplifier gain formulas, Voltage follower (buffer), summing amplifier, Comparator: op-amp as comparator, threshold detection
    - *Real-world Applications*: Analog sensor signal conditioning, ECG/EEG biopotential front-ends, Audio preamplifiers
  - **Unit 4: Digital Electronics Basics** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Number systems: binary, decimal, hexadecimal (conversions), Logic levels (HIGH/LOW, 0/1), Basic logic gates: AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR with truth tables, Boolean algebra: basic laws and De Morgan's theorems, K-map (up to 4 variables), half adder and full adder (truth table and logic diagram)
    - *Real-world Applications*: CPU ALU arithmetic circuits, Data bus multiplexing, Digital registers
  - **Unit 5: Electronic Systems and Emerging Applications** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Microcontroller overview: architecture, GPIO pins, PWM (concept), Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADC): digital ramp and flash type ADC, Communication interfaces: UART, SPI, I2C (overview and uses), Sensors and signal chain: sensor -> conditioning (op-amp) -> ADC -> microcontroller, Wireless communication basics: Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, Zigbee
    - *Real-world Applications*: IoT weather stations, Smart home sensor nodes, Wearable fitness trackers
- **Textbooks & References**:
  - Robert L. Boylestad & Louis Nashelsky, Electronic Devices and Circuit Theory, Pearson
  - Morris Mano, Digital Logic and Computer Design, Prentice Hall
  - D. Roy Choudhury & Shail B. Jain, Linear Integrated Circuits, New Age International
  - Adel S. Sedra & Kenneth C. Smith, Microelectronic Circuits, Oxford University Press

### ACS201 — Essentials of Data Structures
- **Short Title**: Data Structures | **Category**: ESC | **Type**: Theory | **Credits**: 3 | **NCrF Hours**: 45
- **Semesters**: Semester 2
- **Course Objectives**:
  - Understand data structures and why choosing the right one affects program performance.
  - Implement linked lists and perform basic insert, delete, and traverse operations.
  - Implement stack and queue and apply them to simple real-world problems.
  - Apply searching and sorting algorithms and compare their efficiency.
  - Understand trees and graphs at a conceptual level with real-world connections.
- **Course Outcomes (COs)**:
  - **CO1** (K3): Analyze algorithm efficiency and implement 1D and 2D array operations.
  - **CO2** (K3): Implement singly linked list operations and understand doubly and circular linked lists.
  - **CO3** (K3): Implement stack and queue data structures and apply them to expression evaluation.
  - **CO4** (K4): Compare and implement searching and sorting algorithms in C and Python.
  - **CO5** (K2): Understand tree and graph terminology, representations, and traversal concepts.
- **5 Units Breakdown**:
  - **Unit 1: Introduction and Arrays** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Data structure: definition, need, classification (linear / non-linear, static / dynamic), Algorithm efficiency: Big O notation: O(1), O(n), O(log n), O(n^2) (conceptual), Array operations: insert, delete, search, traverse in C/Python, 2D arrays: matrix addition, multiplication, and transpose
    - *Real-world Applications*: NumPy multidimensional tensors, Image matrix representation, Tabular datasets
  - **Unit 2: Linked List** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Node structure: data + pointer; why linked list over array, Singly linked list: insertion (head, tail, position), deletion, traversal, Doubly linked list: concept and structure only, Circular linked list: concept and structure only
    - *Real-world Applications*: Browser forward/backward history (doubly linked list), Music playlist repeat loop (circular), LRU cache
  - **Unit 3: Stack and Queue** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Stack: LIFO principle, push, pop, peek; array-based implementation in C/Python, Applications of stack: balanced parentheses check, function call stack, Queue: FIFO principle, enqueue, dequeue; array implementation in C/Python, Applications of queue: print spooler, task scheduling queue, Polish notation
    - *Real-world Applications*: Ctrl+Z undo stack, Message broker event queue (WhatsApp delivery), CPU process scheduler
  - **Unit 4: Searching and Sorting** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Linear search and Binary search: algorithm, trace, and comparison, Bubble sort and Selection sort: trace and implementation in C/Python, Insertion sort and Quick sort: trace and concept, Merge sort: divide-and-conquer concept and trace, Python built-in sort() and sorted() (Timsort overview)
    - *Real-world Applications*: Database B-Tree indexing, E-commerce catalog ranking, Search engine indexing
  - **Unit 5: Trees and Graphs (Concept Level)** (Weightage: 20%)
    - *Core Topics*: Binary tree terminology: root, leaf, height, depth, level, subtree, Binary Search Tree (BST): properties, insertion, search, Tree traversals: Inorder, Preorder, Postorder traces, Graph terminology: vertex, edge, directed and undirected graphs, Graph representation: adjacency matrix and adjacency list
    - *Real-world Applications*: Social networks (graph), File system directory hierarchy (tree), GPS routing networks
- **Textbooks & References**:
  - Seymour Lipschutz, Data Structures with C (Schaum's Outline), McGraw Hill
  - Ellis Horowitz & Sartaj Sahni, Fundamentals of Data Structures in C, Silicon Press
  - Michael T. Goodrich, Roberto Tamassia & Michael H. Goldwasser, Data Structures and Algorithms in Python, Wiley
  - Thomas H. Cormen et al., Introduction to Algorithms, MIT Press
